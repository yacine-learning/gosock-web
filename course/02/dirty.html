What you should know
  • write to a file copies into the page cache and returns: the pages are dirty, newer than the disk.
  • The kernel writes them back later: after about 30 seconds, or when memory is short, or when asked. fsync(fd) (Go: f.Sync()) waits until one file is on the disk; sync does it for all.
  • "write returned" means "the kernel has it", not "the disk has it".
Why the kernel waits, and what it costs

Waiting lets the kernel group many small writes into a few large ones, and skip writing data that is overwritten or deleted before it reaches the disk. Programs get fast writes.

The price is a window during which a power cut loses data that write said was written. Databases and editors that must not lose your work call fsync at the moments that matter, and accept that it is slow: it waits for the disk.

Run this

$ cp /tmp/big.bin /tmp/copy.bin $ grep -E 'Cached|Dirty' /proc/meminfo $ time sync $ grep Dirty /proc/meminfo

Copy once more, then do nothing for 40 seconds and look again:

$ cp /tmp/big.bin /tmp/copy2.bin $ grep Dirty /proc/meminfo $ sleep 40; grep Dirty /proc/meminfo

What you should notice

  • Right after cp, 2048 kB are dirty: the copy "finished" before the disk saw it.
  • sync took time, because it waited for the disk; then Dirty is 0.
  • Left alone, Dirty also fell to 0, by itself, about 30 seconds later.
