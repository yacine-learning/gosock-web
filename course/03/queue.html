What you should know
  • After listen, the kernel completes handshakes by itself. A finished connection waits in the listening socket's accept queue until the program calls accept.
  • The backlog (listen's second argument) bounds that queue; Linux keeps up to backlog + 1.
  • When it is full, new SYNs are dropped: the client's connect waits and retries, and may time out.
Why a queue, and what ss shows

The queue absorbs bursts: clients that arrive while the program handles another connection are not refused. For a listening socket, ss -tln uses its two columns differently: Recv-Q is the number of connections waiting in the accept queue, Send-Q the backlog.

A queued connection is complete for the client: connect returned, and the client can even send data, which waits in the new socket's receive buffer. If the listener is closed, the kernel resets every queued connection.

The kernel caps the backlog at /proc/sys/net/core/somaxconn (4096). Go asks for that maximum.

Run this

lazy listens on port 9000 with a backlog of 2 and never calls accept. Build it and start it on B, in the background (&):

 $ lazy &

Four clients from A, in the background too, then a look at their sockets:

$ nc $B_IP 9000 < /tmp/hello.txt & $ nc $B_IP 9000 < /tmp/hello.txt & $ nc $B_IP 9000 < /tmp/hello.txt & $ nc $B_IP 9000 < /tmp/hello.txt & $ ss -tan

The listener's queue, then the end of lazy:

$ ss -tln $ pkill lazy $ ss -tan

What you should notice

  • On A, three connections are ESTAB although nobody accepted them; the fourth is stuck in SYN-SENT.
  • B's kernel view shows the accept queue: 3 waiting, each with 21 bytes in its receive buffer.
  • ss -tln on B: Recv-Q 3, Send-Q 2. When lazy died, the three queued connections were reset (nc printed "Connection reset by peer"), and the fourth was refused.
