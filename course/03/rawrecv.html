Write rawrecv: your module 1 receiver, made of socket system calls instead of net.Listen and Accept.
What you should know
  • The calls, in order: Socket, SetsockoptInt(SO_REUSEADDR), Bind, Listen, Accept, then the read loop of rawcopy.
  • An address is a struct: &syscall.SockaddrInet4{Port: 9000}; no Addr means 0.0.0.0, every address of the machine.
  • Two descriptors to close: the listening socket and the connection.

The spec

CommandBehaviour
rawrecv PORT DESTListen on PORT, accept one connection, store every byte in DEST, print received N bytes, exit with status 0.
ErrorsReturned; main prints rawrecv: … and exits with status 1.
RuleNo package net, os.Open/Create or io.Copy: system calls only. The port must be usable at once after an earlier connection on it, even one still in TIME_WAIT.
Why SO_REUSEADDR

A connection ends in TIME_WAIT on the side that closed first, for 60 seconds, and keeps its port during that time. Without SO_REUSEADDR, bind on that port fails with EADDRINUSE until the minute is over. With it, a listening socket may bind next to old TIME_WAIT connections. Go sets it on every listener, which is why gosock recv never met the problem.

Run this

$ rawrecv 9000 big.copy $ gosock send $B_IP:9000 /tmp/big.bin $ sha256sum big.copy $ strace -e trace=network rawrecv 9000 x.bin

(The last command waits: send it something from A, or press Ctrl+C.)

What you should notice

  • The same fingerprint as the original: the receiver is yours down to the system calls.
  • The trace is your code: socket, setsockopt, bind, listen(3, 8), accept.
